A little about Steelebird.

Frequently asked questions about our publishing vision, the books we're looking for, and how we work with authors and agents.

01What is Steelebird building?

Steelebird is a new independent publishing house with a simple ambition: to champion timeless books and singular authors, and to make discovering them part of the fun. We're building something a little different: curated collections inspired by the interests and passions of readers. Each brings together out-of-print treasures, long-lost favorites, and extraordinary new work, with novels and novellas from authors past and present.

We're starting with two collections: American Myths, exploring and reexamining the American experience, and Other Worlds, devoted to thoughtful, philosophical speculative fiction. By bringing books together around shared interests, we can connect with the communities of readers who care about those subjects, take part in their conversations, and give each release a more meaningful introduction.

And the books are only the beginning. We're also developing essays, criticism, videos, and spaces for conversation, creating more ways to explore ideas, discover authors, and follow your curiosity. Ultimately, I want Steelebird to be a place readers return to not just for the books, but for the discoveries and conversations around them.

02Who is Clay Swartz, Steelebird's founder?

I'm Clay Swartz. For about 15 years, I've worked across editorial, operations, and marketing, which has given me a fairly well-rounded perspective on how books and stories find their audiences. I started in newspapers and magazines after studying journalism, so the editorial side of the work has always been central to what I do. Along the way, I created and wrote the WHO WINS? children's book series with Hachette, which gave me a chance to experience publishing from the author's side of the table.

From there, I moved into operations at independent publishers in the Southwest, learning the practical side of the business from the inside: distribution, warehousing, fulfillment, production, and all the moving parts involved in getting books into readers' hands. That experience brought the editorial and business sides of publishing together for me, and ultimately helped give me the confidence to launch Steelebird.

Looking back, I can see how much of my career was preparing me for Steelebird without my realizing it. The editorial instincts, operational experience, relationships, and, especially, my love of finding interesting ways to position stories and connect them with readers have come together at the right time. Steelebird is new, but it feels like a natural next chapter, and I'm building it with every intention of being here for the long haul.

Alongside assembling our first list, we're putting substantial work into Steelebird's brand and visual identity. That's still taking shape, which is why the public website is fairly bare and focused on acquisitions for now. I'd love to show you what we're developing once it's a little further along.

03What kinds of books are you looking for?

We're open to fiction and nonfiction across genres, including literary novels, crime, speculative fiction, unusual nonfiction, and backlist titles with a compelling reason for a new edition. An unfamiliar or unconventional book is often exactly what I want to read.

04How does Steelebird publish its books?

We'll publish standalone titles as well as books within our curated collections. Every edition will receive thoughtful editorial preparation, distinctive design, and a publication plan shaped around the book and its readers. Collections may feature coordinated artwork and additional editorial material, but not every book needs to belong to a collection. We want the format and presentation to serve the work, not the other way around.

05What rights and terms do you offer?

For reprints, we generally offer a limited-term license for U.S. English-language print rights, with a recoupable advance and royalties based on the retail list price, increasing after an agreed sales threshold. I'm very open to discussing the structure and terms that make sense for a particular book and its existing publishing arrangements.

For new work, we'd propose a similar approach, but ideally with rights for hardcover, paperback, ebook, and audio. I understand those rights aren't always available together, and I'm happy to work through what's possible on a title-by-title basis.

06What is your publication timeline and distribution plan?

We generally aim to publish within 18 months of agreement, with the publication schedule developed collaboratively for each title. Our distribution and fulfillment partnerships are in place, giving us the infrastructure to sell directly through Steelebird and reach bookstores and other retailers through established book-trade channels. We'll tailor the launch and marketing plan to each book, with particular attention to reaching the readers and communities most likely to connect with it.

07How do you work with authors and agents?

Authors and agents should be involved in discussions about positioning, design, and launch plans. We'll handle production and publishing responsibilities, and we'll be candid about what a new press can realistically promise. I would rather publish fewer books well than make an offer I can't support.

08How will you share individual proposals?

When we identify a particular title, I'll share a dedicated, access-controlled proposal with the rights under discussion, advance and royalty terms, a working publication plan, and a place for questions and revisions. That space will grow with our conversations. Nothing is currently awaiting review here.
