# Re:Read PhotoEssay / MaterialDetail — photography brief

Status: creative direction only, not yet approved or wired to the page. Images must be real, verified, licensed and credited. Do not imply photographs are of Re:Read production until we have photographs from our own print/bindery partners.

## The narrative
What would make a reader desire a Re:Read edition as a physical object? Show the paper, printing and construction closely enough that a person can practically *feel* it, and explain why the detail matters. Think maker/atelier journalism, not a generic craft moodboard.

## The first eight shots to seek or commission
1. Endpaper being opened: a double-page actual marbled paper with the spine visible, controlled light, full bleed possible.
2. Headband close-up: precise, tactile macro showing colored thread across the head of an actual sewn text block.
3. Paper stock edge: raking side-light on thick uncoated sheets, page fibers and edges visible.
4. Dust-jacket fold: clear close-up of a real jacket being folded, with paper grain/coating readable.
5. Signature sewing: human hands and thread at the signature, process—not an anonymous stack of books.
6. Letterpress or foil/cloth impression: close-up of finish with real material reflecting light, no impossible mock foiling.
7. Printer's sheet: a real printed sheet checked on a press or cutting table.
8. Bindery finishing detail: exposed board, spine lining, or casing-in, showing meaningful construction.

## What makes an image usable
- A clear factual subject that can be named in a helpful caption.
- Shot close enough to reveal material, with a distinct, art-directed crop; vary wider and macro compositions.
- Real photography, sufficient resolution for intended component (target 1600px long edge or better; exceptions by explicit approval).
- Known source and license allowing public commercial use; record photographer, credit, URL, license and any required attribution.
- No watermark, AI/generated image, film still of unknown rights, or invented title/mock cover.
- Vivid but honest texture and color, not sepia/archival nostalgia; should coexist with cream/navy/marble without trying to duplicate it.

## Component design direction
- Begin with **PhotoEssay** as a restrained two- or three-photo editorial grid with one lead image and one accompanying caption, not a moving ribbon. Important images fully visible without clicks, hover, or timed motion.
- Build **MaterialDetail** using one photographed object, one precise explanation of its purpose, and optionally a second zoomed detail.
- Keep labels only when they convey a real material/process term. No numbered "craftsmanship" cards, invented quotes, random eyebrows, parallax, or faux 3-D books.
- If motion earns a role, animate **a single transition between verified details** with direct user control and reduced-motion fallback; the default state must stand alone.
- On mobile, order headline, explanation, key photograph and supporting photographs to support reading, not a long horizontally scrolled visual strip.

## Publishing and legal rules
- These are photography references about fine bookmaking, not proof that Re:Read has specified headbands, dust jackets, paper stocks, or production partners yet.
- Distinguish inspiration, design goals, and confirmed manufacturing specs in copy.
- Do not ship production imagery until provenance and permissions are recorded per asset.

## Acceptance test
A first-time reader should be able to answer: What physical detail am I looking at? Why does it matter? Is this a Re:Read book or a reference? How do I follow the making of the first collection?
